The Story Behind EFE

“You fit here.”

The story behind Equestrian for Everyone (EFE) is deeply personal to Jenn Crawford. It wasn’t started simply as an inclusion initiative—it grew out of Jenn’s own experience of becoming a para equestrian and realizing how difficult it could be to find her place in the sport.

After a devastating spinal injury, Jenn was told she would likely never walk again, let alone ride. Yet only six months later, she was back in the saddle, adapting the way she rode and finding new ways to communicate with her horse.

But returning to riding revealed a problem: Jenn didn’t know where to turn. She struggled to find coaches who understood how to work with a rider with a disability, and she found herself often being the only para rider she knew. As she progressed into jumping, she also encountered questions, uncertainty and concerns about whether someone with a disability could safely participate in the sport.

Instead of accepting those barriers, Jenn decided to create something she wished had existed for her.

Equestrian for Everyone was born from the desire to make sure the next para rider—or any rider who feels like they don’t fit the traditional equestrian mold—doesn’t have to struggle to find their place.

The organization began by bringing para and non-para riders together through clinics and educational opportunities, creating a space where riders could learn from one another, meet other para equestrians, and see what was possible. Jenn’s vision has grown beyond simply creating opportunities for para riders; it is about building one equestrian community where people of all abilities, backgrounds and ages can participate together.

At its heart, EFE exists because Jenn experienced firsthand what it feels like to wonder, “Where do I fit in this sport now?”

Her answer was to build a community where the answer is: “You fit here.”

And that is why the name Equestrian for Everyone matters. It isn’t just a name or a slogan—it represents the reason Jenn started it in the first place: because no one should have to give up their place in the equestrian world simply because their path looks different.
